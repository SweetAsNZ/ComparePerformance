Naval Architecture Formulas & Performance Ratios

The standard hydrodynamics and mathematical definitions used by CatamaranShow and multihull yacht designers.

1. Bruce Number (Multihull Power-to-Weight)

Bruce Number = √[ Sail Area (sq ft) ] / ∛[ Displacement (lbs) ]

Developed by Edmond Bruce, this dimensionless coefficient measures the driving power per pound of displacement. A standard cruising catamaran scores 1.1–1.25, high-performance cruisers score 1.25–1.45, and grand prix racing multihulls exceed 1.45.

2. Derek Kelsall Performance Index

Kelsall Index = [ LWL (ft) × Bruce Number ] / 70

Formulated by legendary multihull pioneer Derek Kelsall. It incorporates the waterline length ($LWL$) into the Bruce Number, recognizing that longer waterlines reduce wave-making resistance and produce higher hull speeds.

3. Sail Area / Displacement (SA/D)

SA/D = Sail Area (sq ft) / [ Displacement (lbs) / 64 ]^(2/3)

The universal yacht design metric for sail horsepower relative to wetted volume. Cruising cats are typically 19–23, performance cruisers 24–30, and high-performance racing catamarans 30–40+.

4. Displacement / Length Ratio (D/L)

D/L = [ Displacement (long tons) ] / [ 0.01 × LWL (ft) ]³

Measures the lightness and fineness of the hulls relative to length. Lower numbers mean less resistance. Cruising cats are 130–180, performance cruisers are 75–125, and ultra-light racers are under 70.

5. Sail Performance (% of Windspeed)

Sail Performance (%) = [ Estimated Boat Speed / True Wind Speed ] × 100

Calculates the percentage of true wind speed a catamaran achieves on a reach under 15 knots of wind. Heavy charter catamarans achieve 60–65%, performance catamarans reach 75–85%, and foiling or ultra-light designs achieve 90–110%+.

6. Bridgedeck Clearance to Length Ratio

Bridgedeck Ratio = Bridgedeck Clearance (m) / LWL (m)

Higher clearance prevents slamming in ocean swells. A clearance ratio above 0.055–0.065 is considered optimal for offshore bluewater comfort and wave passage.
